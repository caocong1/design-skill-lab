## Key facts
1. **arXiv:2607.28439 (2026-07-30), ESPP persona panel for generative UI**: 7,000 screenshots (500 instructions × 14 models, 5 human ratings each); a single-pass judge reaches Pearson 0.716 / Spearman 0.612 against humans, a persona panel 0.922 / 0.904; the leaderboard's "Overall scores span a narrow 3.45–3.73 range". Absolute-scale judging compresses.
2. **arXiv:2605.04165 (2026-05-05), FlowEval** (Wu, Vaithilingam, Schoop, Nichols, Barik): 945 UIs, 429 expert pairwise comparisons; a trace metric reaches Spearman 0.96 over 7 models; Cohen's κ 0.46 / 73.0 % agreement against a pairwise MLLM judge (Claude Opus 4.5: κ 0.44 / 71.1 %); A/B order randomised.
3. **arXiv:2609.34854 (2026-09-28), AUV-Bench**: 1,395 executable web UIs, 12 models, 44 professional designers; the best model–designer Spearman is 0.57; "exact diagnosis-chain success peaks at only 24.7%"; after controlled degradation the overall score is unchanged for 83.5–86.4 % of samples. Judges are insensitive to real regressions.
4. **arXiv:2609.22971 (2026-09-19), AMUSER**: a YES/NO judge's κ against the human majority rises from 0.40 to 0.48 after bias calibration (GPT-5.5 0.591); human Fleiss' κ 0.38 when independent, 0.72 after debate. Positive bias is measurable and partly correctable.
5. **arXiv:2606.05697 (2026-06-04), PerceptUI**: order-swapped pairwise "Consistent Accuracy" 44.30 vs GPT-5 31.09 / Claude Opus 4.6 26.44; position bias measured as the first/second-answer gap (o1: 16.56 vs 97.78); UICrit comment quality 0.54 vs designers 0.75. Pairwise judging needs both orders.
6. **arXiv:2610.11539 (2026-10-08, v2 10-14), Design Creativity Bench**: 15 models × 160 designs; originality 0.592 [0.582, 0.602] across models vs 0.764 human–model; creative range 0.581 vs 0.902 human; "Model-generated designs resemble one another more than they resemble the human reference"; an embedding scorer was used because the LLM judge (highest human agreement) failed monotonicity.
7. **arXiv:2607.22928 (2026-07-24), Design Theater**: 120 interfaces from 5 tools; principle recognition mean 0.54; "four of five tools implementing 6% or fewer functional principles"; a Design Homogeneity Index (visual 0.137, layout 0.196, colour 31.9): tools converge on visual and layout features and vary on colour; accepted at AAAI/AIES (Crossref: AIES vol. 9 no. 2 pp. 1325–1337, 2026-10-11); cited in 2026 by 2609.34854 and 2610.21185.
8. **arXiv:2609.15078 (2026-09-14, v3 10-12), vibe design agents**: typicality-weighted spec sampling lowers screenshot cosine similarity from 0.6765 to 0.5438 (τ = 2.0) and raises option coverage from 1.00 to 2.87; an online RCT over 3.39 M tasks: thumbs-down −14.6 %, CI [−32.8 %, +3.5 %], not significant; the judge is "not validated against human annotation".
9. **arXiv:2603.13036 (2026-03-13), Interrogating Design Homogenization in Web Vibe Coding**: a 63-source review plus 6 tool walkthroughs; proposes "productive friction" (reflective prompting, anchoring to brand design systems, provenance); no measurements.
10. **arXiv:2605.24830 (2026-05-24), Macaron-A2UI**: a model trained on "A2UI v0.8"; A2UI-Bench with 300 tasks, VLM-judged, no human evaluation; 75.6 vs GPT-5.4 74.1.
11. Not found: an arXiv paper for Design Arena (its methodology page describes blind four-model tournaments with Bradley–Terry scoring and hidden identities; vote totals are secondhand), a WebDev Arena paper (blog: "over 80,000 community votes"), or any paper measuring "AI slop" in generated interfaces beyond entries 6 and 7.

12. **arXiv:2604.09876 (v2 2026-09-14), Efficient Personalization of Generative UIs** (full): 20 people judged all 600 pairs of same-prompt generated mobile UIs (12,000 labels): mean pairwise agreement **0.624**, Krippendorff's α **0.248**; four-way strength labels α 0.104. The best preference predictors reach 56–62 %. A ceiling for any judge-versus-human agreement on generated UI.
13. **arXiv:2611.06272 (2026-11-04, IEEE VIS 2026 VISxGenAI workshop, pilot), human–LLM alignment on visualisation preference** (full): 24 chart pairs, 10 people, 5 VLMs sampled 10 times: Jensen–Shannon divergence from the human panel 0.29–0.31 for GPT-4V, GPT-5 Chat, Claude Opus 4.5 and Sonnet 4.5 against 0.08 for a calibrated reference; the four models are more certain than the panel on 21–24 of 24 pairs. Charts, not UIs; tiny.
14. **arXiv:2610.26826 (v2 2026-11-04; also an ICLR 2027 submission), RefCraft** (full): 300 reference-conditioned webpage tasks, 9 models, 10 experts re-rating 100 artefacts. Best 52.12 / 100; a holistic overall rating scores **42.3 points higher** than the keypoint protocol; screenshot-only evidence leaves 437 of 918 core cases unresolved against 26 with executable evidence; evaluator–expert weighted κ 0.64 overall but **0.47 on visual style**; removing the references drops the best model from 52.1 to 17.3.
15. **arXiv:2608.29387 (v2 2026-09-07; EMNLP 2026), EvoGenUI-Bench** (full): 150 five-turn UI tasks, 8 generators, a separate evaluator model: evaluator accuracy 87.5 % with full evidence, 78.3 % without the screenshot and **55.0 % without the interaction trace**; κ 0.73 against humans whose own Fleiss κ is 0.78; best model passes 74.9 % of turns but 37.3 % of five-turn episodes; repeated runs of one model vary by about 3.5 points.
16. **arXiv:2606.16262 (2026-06-15), UXBench** (full): critique reports from 8 judge models applied by a fixed repair agent raise the repaired pages by **+0.176 on a 1–5 scale** (95 % CI 0.140–0.212; experts +0.17 to +0.35 per model); automated and human model rankings correlate at Spearman 0.635 with a CI that includes zero; many UX failures only show when the page is operated.
17. **arXiv:2610.21185 (2026-10-15), Mine, Sample, Render** (webpage section and appendix, full): sampling a design-attribute configuration from a corpus-mined schema and giving it to the model as the brief raises GPT-5.5's screenshot diversity (Vendi) from **2.158 to 3.956** (Kimi K2.6: 2.426 to 3.842); seeded pages are 95.1–97.7 % of what a VLM picks as the odd one out. Diversity only: no quality or human check.
18. **arXiv:2610.10374 (2026-10-07), TaoD2C-Bench** (full): on 2,861 production designs, giving models the expert requirements raises code-structure F1 by 6.8–24.2 points while visual-similarity scores barely move (+0.18); alignment is the weakest dimension (mean F1 23.3). A screenshot match is not structural correctness.
19. **arXiv:2610.35138 (2026-10-26), Co-Design Bench** (full): 273 tasks from real coding sessions: the best model passes 69.9 % of criteria per task but fully satisfies 26.4 % of tasks; the protocol has a second LLM verifier attack every Pass verdict and demands a screenshot for visual claims and recorded actions for behaviour claims (its effect is not quantified).
20. **arXiv:2610.39488 (2026-10-29), InterA11y** (full): on 197 expert-annotated cases from 54 real pages, existing LLM accessibility checkers reach 30–50 % recall on interactive criteria and their false-discovery rate varies by up to 26.5 points with the model; adding rubrics and interactive probing tools lifts recall to 76–79 % at about 93 % precision. Real pages, not generated UIs.
21. **Critique can hurt good output (OpenReview, ICLR 2027 submissions, abstracts only).** "Repair Rate Is Not Repair" (code, one model pair): against a "no issues found" control, a cross-model critic's counterfactual uplift is **−0.041** (95 % CI −0.061 to −0.022); it reports a defect in **94.5 %** of programs that already pass. "Diagnose, Then Select" (maths): a diagnosis fixes 8.8 % of a weaker solver's wrong answers and corrupts 25.7 % of its correct ones.
22. **Designers' side.** arXiv:2611.13211 (2026-11-09; 17 interviews, full): all 17 use prompt-to-design tools, mostly for early ideation; 5 of 17 report generic, repetitive, off-brand output "regardless of the product context"; 2 re-run the same prompt in several windows to get variants. NN/g "Good from Afar, But Far from Good: AI Prototyping in Real Design Contexts" (2025-10-24, reviewed 2026-08-19; qualitative): detailed prompts and a Figma frame beat broad prompts; unspecified style defaults to a generic neutral sans look.
