## Key facts
1. **Aesthetic-usability effect**: attractive design is perceived as more usable. [Polish buys tolerance and can mask problems in testing.]
2. **Choice overload**: too many options overwhelm (paradox of choice). **Hick's law**: decision time grows with the number and complexity of choices. [Fewer, clearer options; progressive disclosure; a recommended default.]
3. **Chunking**: break information into meaningful groups. **Miller's law**: about 7 ± 2 items in working memory; (page) "don't use the magical number seven to justify unnecessary design limitations"; capacity varies with prior knowledge and context.
4. **Working memory** (missing from the 2026-09-21 digest): (page) holds about **4-7 chunks**, each fading after **20-30 s**; support recognition over recall (visited-link styling, breadcrumbs); carry critical information across screens (comparison tables) so the system, not the user, remembers.
5. **Cognitive load**: mental resources needed to understand and use an interface. **Cognitive bias**: systematic errors of judgement. [Remove what does not serve the task; do not exploit biases.]
6. **Doherty threshold**: productivity soars when system and user respond in **< 400 ms**; (page) give feedback within 400 ms, use perceived performance, animation while loading, progress bars (tolerable regardless of accuracy), and a purposeful delay can increase perceived value and trust.
7. **Fitts's law**: time to acquire a target depends on distance and size. [Large targets near the pointer/thumb; screen edges and corners are easy to hit.]
8. **Flow**: full, energised immersion. **Goal-gradient effect**: motivation rises near the goal. [Show progress; remove interruptions from the core task.]
9. **Jakob's law**: users spend most of their time on other sites and expect yours to work the same way. [Convention is a feature.]
10. **Gestalt grouping**: common region (shared bounded area), proximity, Prägnanz (simplest interpretation), similarity, uniform connectedness (visually connected = related). [Group with space first, containers second; keep related things alike.]
11. **Mental model**: people act on a compressed model of how the system works. [Match it or teach it.]
12. **Occam's razor**: prefer the explanation with the fewest assumptions. **Tesler's law**: some complexity cannot be reduced (conservation of complexity). [Let the system carry irreducible complexity, not the user.]
13. **Paradox of the active user**: users never read manuals and start using software immediately. [Guidance in context.]
14. **Pareto principle**: roughly 80 % of effects come from 20 % of causes. **Parkinson's law**: a task inflates to fill the time available. [Optimise the critical few flows; shorten time to complete.]
15. **Peak-end rule**: experiences are judged by their peak and their end. [Design the high point and the finish.]
16. **Postel's law**: be liberal in what you accept, conservative in what you send. [Forgiving inputs, strict outputs.]
17. **Selective attention**: people attend to stimuli related to their goals. **Von Restorff effect**: the item that differs is remembered. [One distinctive primary action; do not style your own UI like an ad.]
18. **Serial position effect**: first and last items are remembered best. [Key items at the ends of lists and navigation.]
19. **Zeigarnik effect**: uncompleted or interrupted tasks are remembered better. [Visible incomplete state invites completion.]

## What it changes for the skills
- skills/critique-design/references/heuristics.md: name the law when justifying a finding (e.g. Fitts for target size and placement, Hick for option count, Doherty for feedback latency, Jakob for convention breaks, serial position for navigation order); it turns taste into an argument.
- skills/design-studio/references/disciplines/product-ui.md: Doherty (< 400 ms feedback), working-memory limits (carry context across steps, recognition over recall) and Tesler (system absorbs complexity) inform flows and forms.
- Pair with NN/g's 10 usability heuristics (reachable on 2026-09-27: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/ , "last reviewed 2024-01-30": visibility of system status; match between system and real world; user control and freedom; consistency and standards; error prevention; recognition rather than recall; flexibility and efficiency of use; aesthetic and minimalist design; help users recognise, diagnose and recover from errors; help and documentation) - names only, not digested here.
