## Key facts — EU
1. **Art. 50 duties** (Art. 50(1)–(5)): (1) providers of AI systems that interact directly with people must inform them they are interacting with AI unless obvious to a "reasonably well-informed, observant and circumspect" person; (2) providers of systems generating synthetic audio, image, video or text must mark outputs **machine-readably** and make them detectable (exempt: assistive standard editing, no substantial alteration); (3) deployers of emotion recognition / biometric categorisation must inform exposed persons; (4) deployers must disclose **deep fakes** (artistic/satirical/fictional works: disclosure "that does not hamper the display or enjoyment") and **AI-generated text published to inform the public on matters of public interest** (exempt if human review/editorial control + a person holds editorial responsibility); (5) information must be "clear and distinguishable", **at the latest at the first interaction or exposure**, and meet accessibility requirements.
2. **Dates.** Art. 50 applies from **2 August 2026** (Art. 113; timeline; guidelines ¶153). AI Omnibus: proposal 19 Nov 2025, political agreement 7 May 2026, Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 signed 8 July 2026, published OJ 24.7.2026, **in force 27 July 2026** (third day after publication; Commission AI Act page + the Regulation). It leaves Art. 50(1)–(5) wording unchanged, replaces Art. 50(7) (the Commission, with the Board's opinion, assesses whether codes of practice are adequate; no implementing act to approve them), and adds a transition only for Art. 50(2) marking (new Art. 111(4)): generative systems placed on the market **before 2 Aug 2026** must comply by **2 December 2026**; for systems that are partly interactive, the Art. 50(1) disclosure is still due from 2 Aug 2026 (guidelines ¶153). Outputs generated/published before 2 Aug 2026 need no retroactive label, but pre-generated text published on/after that date must be labelled (¶154). Re-checked 2026-11-01: the timeline still shows 02 Aug 2026 "Transparency rules (Article 50) start to apply" and 02 Dec 2026 "New prohibitions + Article 50(2) transition apply"; no further amending act found.
3. **Penalties**: up to **EUR 15 000 000 or 3 %** of worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher (lower of the two for SMEs, guidelines ¶152; the Omnibus extends the lower-of rule to small mid-caps, new Art. 99(6a)); EU institutions up to EUR 750 000 (¶152).
4. **Guidelines status**: content approved by C(2026) 5054 final on **20 July 2026**; "formally adopted … at a later date, when all language versions are available. It is only from that moment that these Guidelines will be applicable." Re-checked 2026-11-01: the library page (publication 20 July 2026, last update 31 July 2026) now says "The Commission adopted these guidelines to offer practical guidance…" but still publishes only the 20 July approval Communication (document 131214) and the annexed Guidelines (131215); no later instrument on the Commission pages, and cellar SPARQL finds no OJ item since 2026-07-01 with "transparency obligations" or "AI-generated" in the title. Treat formal adoption as unconfirmed; paragraph numbers below are from the 20 July annex.
5. **Chatbot / agent disclosure — what counts** (guidelines ¶36–37): first-turn statement that the bot is AI; an "AI" label at the top of an email sent by an AI agent; a voice assistant saying so at session start; plain-language banners (e.g. "You are interacting with an AI system"); **persistent badges** through the interaction; place disclosures **near the input/output field**; periodic spoken reminders in long calls; persistent icons, coloured frames or recognisable "AI" symbols; standardised indicators across a provider's services are recommended. Earcons alone are not sufficient.
6. **What does not count alone** (¶38): disclosure only in T&Cs, URLs or documentation; invisible watermarks/metadata; vague signals ("assistant") or **human-like representations that may mislead**; blanket notices ("Services on this website use AI"); technology-only statements ("this system uses LLMs").
7. **Frequency** (¶39–40): one prominent notice before the first interaction usually suffices; add periodic, context-aware reminders for children, the elderly, people with disabilities or low literacy, emotionally sensitive or immersive use, AI companions, financial/insurance/legal/health advice, complaints handling, and **AI agents acting for the user**; the system must disclose whenever asked about its nature. Avoid "banner blindness". Children-facing notices: child-friendly, succinct, in the local official language, possibly graphics/characters (fn 40).
8. **"Clear and distinguishable"** (¶142–143): noticeable, easy to understand, accessible; separable from surrounding content; not "hidden under layers of menu options" or in manuals. Content labels apply to **every output and every person exposed**; if viewers may join mid-content, repeat later.
9. **Code of Practice on Transparency of AI-generated Content**: timeline on the Code page (last update 19 October 2026): first draft 17 December 2025, second draft 3 March 2026, working-group round 16–27 March 2026, **final code 10 June 2026**. The Commission "concluded on July 8" (opinion page, published 2026-07-09) that the Code "adequately covers the obligations provided for in Articles 50(2), (4) and (5) AI Act", and the AI Board adopted its Adequacy Assessment "the following day"; "Adherence to the code does not constitute conclusive evidence of compliance"; updates considered "at least every two years". Voluntary. Signatories page (last update 24 September 2026): **Section 1: 95, Section 2: 192**, "remains open for signature" (the 2026-09-27 digest said "~190"). Section 1 = providers (Art. 50(2)); Section 2 = deployers (Art. 50(4)).
10. **Provider marking** (Code §1, Measure 1.1): at least **two machine-readable layers** — digitally signed, time-stamped metadata + an imperceptible watermark (free-form text: watermark only, required above 200 tokens); fingerprinting/logging optional and never sufficient alone; do not strip existing provenance metadata from inputs (Measure 1.2).
11. **Label design** (Code §2, Measure 1.1): main element = capitalised **"AI"** (national-language acronym only if language law requires), letters of equal height, proportions kept when resized; encouraged second layer saying "generated" or "modified" and what was changed; any size/colour/typography allowed if clear and distinguishable. Audio-only: short plain-language audible disclaimer **at the beginning** (content language or English); earcons allowed only with awareness measures until an EU audio solution exists. Accessibility: audio description of visual labels, haptic cue before audio deepfakes, high contrast, screen-reader detectable; EN 301 549 / WCAG 2.1 encouraged.
12. **Label placement** (Code §2, Measure 1.2): perceivable without user action; visible long enough; embedded in the content or an equivalent UI overlay; spaced from other overlays and legible on any background. Image/video: where nothing overlaps it, **e.g. top-right corner**; video: at the start, at regular intervals where possible, and at least after interruptions (ad breaks); audio deepfake with a screen: add the visual icon; published text: above/at the top, near the headline or in the opening colophon, may label only the AI part; very short text: a contextual indicator next to the output or a session-start notice. Artistic works: may sit outside but adjacent to the frame, in credits, notes, or UI (Commitment 3). Human-review exception needs a named editorially responsible person with published contact (Commitment 4).
13. **EU icons** (icons page; Code Annex 1): three icons — **basic "AI"**, **fully AI-generated ("AI + GENERATED")**, **partially modified ("AI + MODIFIED")**; four variants: black, white, black 50 % transparent, white 50 % transparent; SVG and PNG zips; free, no attribution. User tests: icons **with a text label performed better**. Display summary: visible at first exposure, no overlapping elements, embedded (or UI overlay), **still visible when reshared or downloaded**, clearly visible size, plain words, no abbreviation except "AI", alt text/ARIA, timed labels long enough for users with cognitive difficulties, a second layer navigable with assistive technologies. Non-signatory use does not signal adherence. The 2026-09-25 Wayback snapshot could not be fetched, so the exact edit is unverified.
13a. **Signatory Task Forces** (page read 2026-11-01): established 30 September 2026 "by over 90 signatories", chaired by the AI Office; second plenaries 1 October 2026. TF1 Marking & Detection: interoperability, standardised interfaces, provider-neutral solutions, public benchmarks, red-teaming; its ad-hoc Interoperability WG met 23 October 2026 (next meeting 5 November 2026), goal "allow laypersons to easily assess whether content is AI-generated or manipulated". TF2 Labelling: "promoting and updating the EU icon (including an audio-only version)", an interactive second layer, common disclosure practices, sectoral practices; ad-hoc WGs: Interactive Second Layer (an element "triggered when the user hovers over or clicks" the icon; launch January 2027), **EU Audio Earcon** ("short tune, combined with spoken language or on its own"; launch January 2027), Sectorial Practices (October–November 2026). Rules of procedure: Vademecum (document 133407); minutes 134454–134456. Design consequence: the audio earcon and the hover/click second layer are being specified now; leave room for them, do not invent a house version.

## Key facts — China
14. **《人工智能生成合成内容标识办法》** 国信办通字〔2025〕2号, signed 2025-03-07 by CAC, MIIT, MPS, NRTA; published 2025-03-14; **in force 2025-09-01**; 14 articles. Explicit label (显式标识) = perceivable text, sound or graphic in the content or interaction UI; implicit label (隐式标识) = technical marks in file data (Art. 3).
15. **Where explicit labels go** (Art. 4; applies to services under 深度合成管理规定 Art. 17(1), i.e. that may confuse the public): text — start, end or suitable middle position, or prominent notice in the interaction UI / around the text; audio — voice or rhythm cue at start/end/middle, or UI notice; image — prominent mark at a suitable position; **video — on the opening frame and around playback** (end/middle optional); virtual scenes — on the opening screen (optionally during service). **Download, copy and export must keep the explicit label** in the file.
16. **Implicit label** (Art. 5): metadata with content attribute, provider name/code, content ID; watermarks encouraged, not mandatory (Q&A: text implicit labels and multimedia watermarks deliberately not required for cost reasons).
17. **Platforms and stores** (Art. 6–7): distribution platforms check metadata and add a prominent notice **around the published item** in three grades — confirmed ("属于"), user-declared ("可能"), detected traces ("疑似"); must offer a "declare AI content" function; in all three cases they also write propagation elements (attribute, platform name/code, content ID) into the file metadata; app stores must ask whether an app provides generative services and check its labelling materials.
18. **User agreement and opt-out** (Art. 8–10): label methods and styles must be stated in the user agreement; unlabelled output only on user request, with the user's duties in the agreement and logs kept **≥ 6 months**; no malicious removal, alteration, forgery or hiding of labels.
19. **GB 45438-2025** 《网络安全技术 人工智能生成合成内容标识方法》, mandatory national standard, published 2025-02-28, **effective 2025-09-01**, ICS 35.030 / CCS L80. Text label wording must contain **"人工智能" or "AI"** plus **"生成" and/or "合成"** (§5.1–5.6).
20. **GB explicit-label specs**: text (§5.1) — text form (both elements) or **corner mark (角标) containing "AI"**, at start/end/middle, legible font and colour; image (§5.2) — text at **edge or corner**, text height **≥ 5 % of the shortest side** (non-rectangular: bounding rectangle); audio (§5.3) — voice label with both elements or rhythm **"short-long short-short" (Morse for "AI")**, at start/end/middle; in voice assistants, customer service and navigation "start/end" means each interaction round; normal speech rate (note: 120–160 Chinese characters/min); video (§5.4) — text on the **opening frame**, edge or corner, **≥ 5 % of shortest side**, **≥ 2 s** at normal speed (end/middle optional); virtual scene (§5.5) — opening screen edge/corner, ≥ 5 %; **interaction UI (§5.6)** — text with both elements, **persistently shown near the content** and/or **persistently at the top, bottom or background** of the interface. Annex D examples: "AI生成" at the foot of a chat bubble; "以上内容由人工智能生成合成" under the composer; a diagonal repeating background watermark. Annex C (informative) gives the content-label examples, Annex D (informative) the interaction-UI examples. Annex B (informative) lists typical scenarios for explicit labels among services "that may cause public confusion or misidentification": chat/writing that imitates a person, voice cloning, face generation/swap/manipulation, immersive scenes, text-to-image, music, text/image-to-video.
21. **GB implicit label** (§6, Annex E, normative): metadata extension field whose name contains **"AIGC"**, value `{"AIGC":{"Label","ContentProducer","ProduceID","ReservedCode1","ContentPropagator","PropagateID","ReservedCode2"}}`; `Label` (a string) = "1" (is AI-generated), "2" (possibly), "3" (suspected); every value is a string that should consist mainly of GB 18030 code points 0x21, 0x23–0x5B, 0x5D–0x7E (printable single-byte characters other than `"` and `\`) plus the escape `\"`; only **one** metadata implicit label per file; content watermarks permitted, not required.
22. **Enforcement** (CAC). 2025-11-25: apps summoned, ordered to fix or removed from stores for missing explicit labels, exports without labels, missing/irregular metadata, platforms not verifying metadata or showing notices, and no declaration function. **2026-04-28**: "剪映"App, "猫箱"App and the "即梦AI" website found "未有效落实人工智能生成合成内容标识规定要求"; measures "约谈、责令改正、警告、从严处理责任人" (basis 《网络安全法》《生成式人工智能服务管理暂行办法》《人工智能生成合成内容标识办法》). **2026-09-02** "清朗·整治AI应用乱象" phase 2: "561万余条" items removed, "4.9万余个" accounts, "2400余个" websites and apps; 豆包、元宝、千问、文心一言 told to "强化AI生成合成内容标识要求落地"; Huawei, Xiaomi, OPPO and vivo stores to tighten developer admission and re-test listed apps. **2026-09-15** 执法典型案例 case 9: a WeChat mini program offering AI chat and image generation had no explicit labels, no implicit metadata label ("属性信息、服务提供者名称或者编码、内容编号"), unlabelled exports and no security assessment → "依法责令下线该小程序" (basis 深度合成管理规定, 标识办法, 安全评估规定). TC260 网安秘字〔2026〕118号 (2026-09-15) issued four 《人工智能应用安全指引》 practice guides (总则 / 教育 / 卫生健康 / 广播电视和网络视听); the notice page does not mention 标识 and the PDFs were not read. No new 标识 national standard or 实践指南 found since 2026-09-27.

## What it changes for the skills
- skills/design-studio/references/disciplines/ai-experience.md: make a **disclosure and labelling spec** a required deliverable for AI products and generated media: EU first-turn statement + persistent badge near the composer; CN persistent "AI生成"-type text near each AI output or a persistent interface notice; disclose on request; reminders for agents, companions and advice contexts; avoid human-like personas that mislead.
- skills/design-studio/references/process/image-generation.md: generated rasters that ship to users carry an explicit label (CN: "AI生成" corner text ≥5 % of shortest side; EU deepfakes: "AI" icon, top-right, survives download) and provenance metadata; exports keep the label; the lab's own comps are marked as generated.
- skills/design-studio/references/disciplines/graphics.md: poster/social/video templates reserve a label slot (corner, clear background, ≥5 % shortest side; video ≥2 s at open and after breaks; EU icon variants black/white/50 %).
- skills/design-studio/references/disciplines/motion.md: timed labels stay ≥2 s (CN video) and long enough for cognitive accessibility (EU).
- skills/design-studio/references/fundamentals/accessibility.md: label accessibility (contrast, alt/ARIA, AT-navigable second layer, audio/haptic equivalents); add AI-labelling rows to the legal matrix.
- skills/design-studio/references/platforms/README.md (+ mini-programs.md): CN app stores check AI-labelling materials at listing; user agreement must describe label style; enforcement has reached mini programs and export paths (fact 22), so a mini-program AI feature draws its labels and its export state like an app.
