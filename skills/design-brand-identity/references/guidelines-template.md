# Brand Guidelines Outline

Before writing, open three real guidelines from a comparable industry
(`../../design-studio/references/resources/brand.md` > Brand guideline
collections) and compare their contents. Then write the shortest document that
lets a stranger apply the identity correctly. Deliver it as a web page or a PDF
**with downloadable assets**; rules without files are decoration.

## 0. Quick Start (one page)

The logo files to use, the two or three colours, the typeface, the three rules
most often broken, where to download everything, who to ask. Most readers stop
here - make it sufficient.

## 1. Brand Foundation

- What the organisation does, for whom, and the one thing it should be known
  for.
- Personality: three to five attributes as "X, not Y".
- How the identity expresses them (one paragraph; no manifesto).

## 2. Logo

- The mark and its idea, in a sentence.
- Lockups: primary, stacked, symbol only, wordmark only; bilingual lockup if
  the brand uses two scripts.
- Colour versions: full colour, one colour (dark), reversed (light), and when
  to use each.
- Clear space, defined by a unit taken from the mark (for example the height
  of a letter).
- Minimum sizes per lockup, in px and mm; the simplified small-size version.
- Placement: preferred positions, co-branding and partner lockups, use on
  photographs.
- Misuse: show six to eight real mistakes (stretching, recolouring, effects,
  low contrast, crowding, rotating, outdated versions, recreating in a
  different typeface).
- App icon, favicon and social avatar crops.

## 3. Colour

- Primary, secondary, neutral and functional colours with names.
- Values for every medium: sRGB hex and OKLCH for screens; CMYK and spot
  references marked "to be proofed" for print.
- Proportions: how much of each colour a typical layout uses.
- Accessible pairings: a table of text-on-background combinations with
  computed contrast ratios; pairings that are forbidden.
- Dark-mode palette.
- Data-visualisation palette if the brand publishes charts.

## 4. Typography

- Families and their roles (display, text, mono), with licence and where to
  obtain them; fallback stacks; the CJK companion and mixed-script rules for
  bilingual brands.
- The type scale with sizes, line heights, weights and tracking.
- Setting rules: alignment, measure, capitalisation, numerals, punctuation.
- Hierarchy examples in real layouts; what not to do.

## 5. Graphic Language

- The device derived from the mark (shape, grid, line, crop) and how to use
  and not overuse it.
- Patterns, textures, gradients.
- Layout principles: grids, margins, the relationship of type and image.

## 6. Imagery

- Photography: subject, light, colour treatment, composition, what to avoid;
  sourcing and licensing.
- Illustration: style, line, palette, level of detail, where it is used.
- Iconography: the family, stroke, sizes, how to extend it.
- Rules for AI-generated imagery, if the brand permits it.

## 7. Motion

- Personality in a sentence; durations, easing or spring tokens.
- Logo animation and its still fallback.
- Transitions for video and UI; sound, if any.

## 8. Voice

- Principles with "we say / we do not say" examples.
- Tone by context (marketing, product UI, support, legal).
- Terminology, product names, capitalisation, number and date formats.
- Bilingual conventions and the translation glossary.

## 9. Applications

Show, do not describe: website header and landing section, product UI screen,
app icon on a home screen, social avatar and post templates, Open Graph image,
slide master, email signature, business card and letterhead, merchandise or
signage if relevant. Each as a rendered mock-up.

## 10. Assets and Governance

- Download links: logo package (SVG, PNG, PDF), colour tokens (CSS, JSON,
  ASE), fonts or where to license them, templates (slides, social), icon set.
- File naming convention and folder structure.
- Version, date, changelog, owner and contact; how to request exceptions.
- Legal: trademark notice, third-party usage terms, co-branding approval.

## Writing the Rules

State each rule as an instruction with a reason and an example ("Keep clear
space of one x-height on every side so the mark never reads as part of
another element"). Prefer a do / don't pair to a paragraph. If a rule cannot
be checked, it is an aspiration - cut it or make it checkable.
