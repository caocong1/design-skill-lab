## 1. Foundation

- What <brand> does, for whom, and the one thing it should be known for.
- Personality: <three to five "X, not Y" pairs>.
- How the identity expresses them: <one paragraph, no manifesto>.

## 2. Logo

- The mark and its idea, in one sentence.
- Lockups: primary, stacked, symbol only, wordmark only; bilingual lockups (<Chinese first / Latin
  first>, horizontal and stacked) if the brand uses two scripts.
- Colour versions: full colour, one colour (dark), reversed (light), and when each is used.
- Clear space: <unit taken from the mark, for example the height of a letter> on every side.
- Minimum sizes per lockup, in px and mm, and the simplified small version below them.
- Placement: preferred positions, on photographs, co-branding and partner lockups.
- Misuse, shown as images: <six to eight real mistakes: stretching, recolouring, effects, low
  contrast, crowding, rotating, outdated versions, re-typesetting the name in another font>.
- App icon, favicon and avatar crops.

## 3. Colour

| Name | Role | Hex | OKLCH | CMYK / spot (to proof) | Share of a typical layout |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <name> | <primary> | <#...> | <oklch(...)> | <...> | <~%> |

- Accessible pairings: text-on-background combinations with computed contrast ratios; forbidden pairs.
- Dark-mode palette. Data-visualisation palette if the brand publishes charts.

## 4. Typography

- Families and roles (display, text, mono), licence and source, fallback stacks; the CJK companion
  and the mixed-script rules.
- Type scale: sizes, line heights, weights, tracking.
- Setting rules: alignment, measure, capitalisation, numerals, punctuation (Chinese punctuation and
  CJK-Latin spacing for bilingual brands). Hierarchy examples in real layouts; what not to do.

## 5. Graphic language

- The device derived from the mark (shape, grid, line, crop): how to use it and when not to.
- Patterns, textures, gradients; layout principles (grids, margins, type and image).
- Generative elements, if any: the fixed core, the parameters and their ranges, the generator link,
  and where only the canonical version may appear.

## 6. Imagery

- Photography: subject, light, colour treatment, composition, what to avoid, sourcing and licences.
- Illustration: style, line, palette, level of detail, where it is used.
- Iconography: the family, stroke, sizes, how to extend it.
- Generated imagery: whether it is allowed, for what, and how it is labelled and recorded.

## 7. Motion

- Personality in one sentence; duration or spring tokens.
- Logo animation and its still fallback; reduced-motion behaviour.
- Transitions for video and UI; sound, if any.

## 8. Voice

- Principles with "we say / we do not say" examples; tone by context (marketing, product UI,
  support, legal).
- Terminology, product names, capitalisation, number and date formats; bilingual conventions and
  the translation glossary.

## 9. Hosted surfaces

- Chat hosts and embedded panes: which host shows the logo and name, where brand colour is allowed
  (primary buttons, badges), host fonts instead of the brand face, voice as the main carrier.
- Mini programs and super-app surfaces: avatar crop, name length limits, capsule keep-out zone.

## 10. Applications

Rendered mock-ups, not descriptions: website header and landing section, a product screen, app icon
on a home screen, social avatar and post templates, Open Graph image, slide master, email
signature, business card and letterhead, merchandise or signage if relevant.

## 11. Assets and governance

- Downloads: logo package (SVG, PNG, PDF), colour tokens (CSS, JSON), fonts or where to license
  them, templates (slides, social), icon set.
- File naming and folder structure.
- Version, date, changelog, owner, contact; how to request an exception.
- Legal: trademark notice, third-party terms, co-branding approval. Clearance status: <cleared by
  counsel on date, markets, classes | clearance outstanding>.
