# Laws of UX

> Source: https://lawsofux.com ｜ Author: Jon Yablonski ｜ Published: living site ｜ Fetched: 2026-09-21 ｜ Method: curl + text extraction of the index page
> This file is a **paraphrased structured digest**, not a verbatim copy. Each law has its own page with origins and takeaways.

## Key rules / claims (design implication in brackets)

- **Aesthetic-usability effect**: attractive design is perceived as more usable. [Polish buys tolerance, and can hide problems in testing.]
- **Choice overload / Hick's law**: decision time grows with the number and complexity of choices. [Fewer, clearer options; progressive disclosure; a recommended default.]
- **Chunking / Miller's law**: working memory holds about 7 +/- 2 items. [Group content; do not treat 7 as a menu limit.]
- **Cognitive load**: the mental effort an interface demands. [Remove what does not serve the task.]
- **Doherty threshold**: productivity rises when system and user respond within about 400 ms. [Feedback fast; use perceived-performance techniques.]
- **Fitts's law**: time to hit a target depends on distance and size. [Large targets, close to where the pointer or thumb already is; edges and corners are easy.]
- **Goal-gradient effect**: motivation increases near the goal. [Show progress.]
- **Jakob's law**: users expect your product to work like the others they know. [Convention is a feature.]
- **Common region, proximity, similarity, uniform connectedness, Praegnanz**: the grouping principles. [Group with space first, then containers; keep related things alike.]
- **Mental model**: people act on what they believe the system does. [Match it or teach it.]
- **Occam's razor / Tesler's law**: prefer the simplest explanation; some complexity is irreducible and someone must carry it. [Let the system carry it, not the user.]
- **Paradox of the active user**: people start using, never read manuals. [Guidance in context.]
- **Pareto principle**: most effect comes from a minority of causes. [Optimise the critical few flows.]
- **Parkinson's law**: tasks expand to fill the time. [Reduce time to complete.]
- **Peak-end rule**: experiences are judged by their peak and their end. [Design the high point and the finish.]
- **Postel's law**: be liberal in what you accept, conservative in what you send. [Forgiving inputs, strict outputs.]
- **Selective attention / Von Restorff effect**: people filter to their goal; the different item is remembered. [One distinctive primary action; do not make ads of your own UI.]
- **Serial position effect**: first and last items are remembered best. [Put key items at the ends.]
- **Zeigarnik effect, Flow, Cognitive bias**: also listed.
